Thesis: discreet wearables and women's health

Health tech you wear like jewelry

Women are already the core customer of the smart ring. The next wave makes health tracking disappear into what women already wear: slimmer rings, ring covers and jackets, smart earrings, and the women's health software and care partnerships that turn data into answers.

Key risks

Why now

The wrist was built for workouts. Women want tracking that fits a ring stack, an ear, and a cycle.

Oura is a women's health company in disguise

Oura's S-1 showed about 72% of members are women and more than half report at least one chronic condition. It has added cycle, pregnancy, perimenopause, and birth control features, plus a women's health AI model launched in February 2026.

Partnerships with Maven Clinic, Midi Health, Mira, Progyny, and Dexcom turn the ring into a front door to care.

Form factor is the product

Oura launched Ring 5, its slimmest yet, and ceramic editions marketed as fashion first. A cottage industry of covers, jackets, and stacking rings followed, from Amazon multipacks to a handmade diamond Cover Ring from Milan.

Earrings are the next frontier. University of Washington researchers found the earlobe is a strong spot to read skin temperature, which matters for ovulation tracking.

Underfunded but rising

Women's health drew a record $1.55B of equity in 2025, yet the WEF and BCG say it still gets only about 6% of private healthcare investment.

AI raises valuations

SVB finds AI-enabled women's health startups command a median $35M pre-money, about triple their non-AI peers.

Subscription models stick

Oura reported about 85% 12-month paid member retention, the benchmark for any new device.

Regulation

The FDA just widened what a wellness wearable can say, which lowers the bar for new devices.

FDA timeline

  • 2025FDA said products estimating blood pressure were not low-risk wellness products.
  • January 6, 2026Revised General Wellness guidance: non-invasive wearables may estimate blood pressure, oxygen saturation, glucose, and HRV as wellness outputs if they avoid diagnosis.
  • January 23, 2026Draft guidance set clinical testing expectations for cuffless blood pressure devices that do make medical claims.

Why it matters: earrings and rings can ship wellness features faster, but anything that diagnoses still needs clearance.

Patent wars decide who can sell

Oura's ITC case kept Ultrahuman's Ring Air out of the U.S. for months in 2025, leaving Oura with about 85% of U.S. smart ring share. Several rivals still face possible import bans.

Accuracy and privacy

Users filed a class action in August 2026 alleging Oura cannot accurately measure sleep cycles. Reproductive health data is among the most sensitive data a company can hold.

Market and outlook

The ring category is small in dollars but compounding fast, and one company shows what scale looks like.

Fastest growth: rings

Smart rings are forecast to grow about 22.5% a year through 2033.

Biggest pool: the platform

Oura alone did $1.21B in nine months; membership revenue grew 121%.

Most open: earrings and jewelry

Earrings and fine-jewelry layers have pull from women and almost no scaled players.

Investment thesis

The next billion-dollar health wearable will be worn like jewelry. Women are already the core customer, and they will pay for devices that disappear into what they already wear and for insights built on female physiology. The early-stage opportunity sits around the ring: new form factors, the jewelry layer, and women's health software and care that turn data into answers.

New form factors

Earrings read temperature well and fit how women already dress.

The jewelry layer

Covers and jackets ride Oura's 5.7M members with fashion margins and little R&D.

Cycle to menopause insights

Female physiology is the data moat most wearables still ignore.

Care partnerships as distribution

Oura plus Maven, Midi, and Mira shows how devices reach patients.

A friendlier FDA

January 2026 guidance lets wellness devices show more metrics without clearance.

Subscription-free challengers

Ultrahuman and Samsung pressure Oura's membership model from below.

Investment criteria

  • Validation against clinical gold standards
  • 12-month retention (Oura's ~85% is the bar)
  • Subscription attach rate
  • Design and fashion credibility
  • Female physiology datasets
  • Data privacy posture
  • Freedom to operate on patents

Outside the thesis

  • Me-too rings exposed to ITC import bans
  • Hardware with no software or data moat
  • Devices edging into diagnosis without clearance

Open opportunities

Smart earrings and pendants built for cycle and perimenopause tracking. Fine-jewelry and fashion brands designed around the major rings. Software that turns ring data into fertility, menopause, and cardiometabolic guidance for women. Care partnerships where a device is the entry point.
