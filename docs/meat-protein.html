Thesis: the return of meat protein

Real meat is back

New dietary guidelines nearly doubled the protein target, GLP-1 users are chasing protein per bite, and shoppers want short ingredient lists. Meat sticks are winning, organ meat is the frontier, and plant-based meat is the cautionary tale.

By Sarina Sherman, Columbia Business SchoolUpdated October 27, 2026Refreshed weekly

Key risks

Why now

Three forces landed at once: a policy shift, a medication shift, and a label shift.

Protein became the organizing principle

The 2025 to 2030 Dietary Guidelines raised the protein target to 1.2 to 1.6 grams per kilogram of body weight, about 81 to 109 grams a day for a 150-pound adult, and told Americans to eat protein at every meal from sources that now explicitly include red meat.

Fortune and Bank of America called meat snacks the clear winner of the GLP-1 shift, growing 6.6% in 2025 while popcorn declined.

Short labels win

Grass-fed meat snack volumes grew 81% in 2025 (Circana). Jack Link's now markets three-ingredient sticks, and Archer grew 57% with clean-label, culinary flavors.

Plant-based meat moved the other way. NIQ's Chris Costagli says shoppers read plant-based labels and found unfamiliar ingredients, added sugar, and high sodium.

Sticks beat jerky on value

Meat sticks average $1.26 an ounce vs. $1.80 for jerky, about 30% cheaper, and come individually wrapped.

Demand outruns supply

Chomps said it could fill only about 85% of orders while producing 2 million sticks a day.

Organ meat goes mainstream

Search interest in beef organ supplements has grown many times over since 2024, led by ancestral-diet influencers.

Regulation and policy

Policy turned from a headwind into a tailwind for animal protein in a single release.

2025 to 2030 Dietary Guidelines

  • January 7, 2026USDA and HHS released the new guidelines with an inverted pyramid that puts protein, full-fat dairy, and healthy fats first.
  • ProteinTarget raised from 0.8 to 1.2 to 1.6 g per kg per day, at every meal.
  • Red meatNow named within recommended protein foods.
  • Saturated fatThe 10% of calories cap stays, which critics say clashes with the red meat message.

Industry response

The Meat Institute and National Cattlemen's Beef Association welcomed the guidelines. The American Heart Association urged people to keep prioritizing plant proteins, seafood, and lean meats.

Supplements and labels

Organ supplements are sold as dietary supplements, so they can make structure and function claims but not disease claims. Grass-fed and clean-label claims are where brands compete, and where substantiation matters.

Market and outlook

Two curves tell the story: animal protein snacks compounding, plant-based meat shrinking.

Fastest growth: sticks

Meat sticks are forecast to grow faster than any other meat snack format, at about 7% a year globally.

Biggest pool: protein snacking

U.S. meat snacks reached $5.5B, about 7% of a $74.9B savory snack market (BofA).

Most open: women, kids, organ meat

Female-focused sticks, lunchbox formats, and organ nutrition in easy forms have few scaled leaders.

Deal flow

Most meat snack capital moves through private equity, strategic launches, and co-manufacturing deals rather than venture rounds, so sizes are rarely disclosed.

Who's already writing checks

Consumer funds and PE are moving in, and co-manufacturers are investing alongside brands.

Bullish

New York consumer investor and marketing firm; invested in female-founded Singing Pastures in April 2026.

GroundForce Capital

Founder-led fund behind Liquid Death, OWYN, and Vive Organic; backed Righteous Felon.

Swander Pace Capital

Food-focused PE that acquired Mighty Spark with Branch Brook Holdings and installed a former Jack Link's sales chief as CEO.

Western Smokehouse Partners

Co-manufacturer that co-invested in a 300,000 sq ft plant dedicated to Chomps.

Landmark Snacks

Co-manufacturer building Chomps' Nebraska plant, financed with regional banks.

Benestar Brands

Holding company that bought 4505 Meats to expand beyond meat snacks.

What the big players are doing

Incumbents are launching their own clean-label sticks, which usually comes before they start buying.

PepsiCo

Launched Good Warrior beef sticks in March 2026, its first standalone meat snack brand.

Jack Link's

Repositioning around three-ingredient sticks after losing 573 basis points of share in 2025.

Kroger

Rolled out Simple Truth clean-label beef sticks among 24 new protein products.

Tyson

Exited plant-based meat entirely, concluding demand did not justify investment.

Beyond

Dropped 'meat' from its name in March 2026 and moved into protein drinks.

Impossible Foods

CEO stepped down; the company floated a hybrid burger made half from beef.

Who's shaping the conversation

Investment thesis

Protein is now the organizing principle of the American diet, and shoppers want it from simple, recognizable sources. The winners turn real meat into convenient daily formats, own enough production capacity to meet demand, and reach the women and families driving protein snacking.

Clean label takes share

Insurgents like Chomps and Archer are taking share from Jack Link's with short ingredient lists.

Capacity is the moat

When the leader can fill only 85% of orders, owning or locking up co-manufacturing is a real advantage.

Women and kids are the growth segments

Singing Pastures, New Primal's lunchbox minis, and Primal Queen target buyers legacy brands ignored.

Organ meat needs better formats

Capsules got it started; gummies, blends, and familiar foods will take it mainstream.

Beyond beef

Chicken, turkey, and pork sticks widen the category and ease beef cost pressure.

Strategics will buy

PepsiCo entered by launching; history says the next step is acquiring a proven brand.

Investment criteria

  • Velocity vs. category growth
  • Repeat and subscribe rates
  • Contracted co-manufacturing capacity
  • Raw material sourcing and cost
  • Ingredient count
  • Share of sales to women and kids
  • Retail door growth

Outside the thesis

  • Plant-based meat analogs
  • Commodity jerky without a brand
  • Organ supplements making disease claims

Open opportunities

Female-founded and female-focused meat sticks. Organ meat in gummies, blends, and everyday foods. Poultry and pork sticks with real flavor. Protein snacks designed for GLP-1 users who need protein in small portions.
