Thesis: fiber and gut health

Fiber is the next protein

Most Americans miss their fiber target while GLP-1 use, endurance training, and AI tracking make the gap visible. The winners make clinically credible fiber easy to take every day through formulation or ingredient IP, not grams alone.

The science

Fiber is the part of plant carbohydrates your body can't digest. It either adds bulk, forms a gel that slows digestion, or gets fermented by gut bacteria into short-chain fatty acids. Different fibers do different jobs, which is why formulation matters more than grams.

U.S. adults meeting the daily fiber target
Meet targetUpper estimateFall short

5 to 10 in 100. Average intake is about 15 g a day against a 28 g Daily Value.

The evidence base

The landmark study is a 2019 WHO-commissioned Lancet review by Reynolds and colleagues: 185 prospective studies covering about 135 million person-years, plus 58 clinical trials. People eating the most fiber had 15 to 30% lower all-cause and cardiovascular mortality, along with less heart disease, stroke, type 2 diabetes, and colorectal cancer.

The benefit peaked around 25 to 29 grams a day, with signs that more is better. Trials also showed lower body weight, blood pressure, and cholesterol.

What's new since then

Diversity over dose. A six-week randomized trial run by ZOE and King's College London found a 30-plus plant blend improved gut microbiome diversity more than single-strain probiotics. The industry calls the shift from more fiber to more kinds of fiber "fibermixxing."

GLP-1 gap. GLP-1 drugs cut appetite, so users eat less fiber exactly when constipation is a common side effect. That makes fiber a natural companion product.

Three mechanisms to know

MechanismWhat it doesExample fibers
BulkingAdds stool mass and speeds transitCellulose, wheat bran, psyllium
Viscous gelSlows digestion; blunts glucose spikes; lowers LDL cholesterol; drives satietyPsyllium, beta-glucan, glucomannan, pectin
FermentationFeeds gut bacteria, producing short-chain fatty acids (the "prebiotic" effect)Inulin, GOS, resistant starch, arabinoxylan, acacia

Why tolerance is the product

Highly fermentable fibers like inulin can cause gas and bloating at higher doses, which kills repeat purchase. Gastroenterologists tell consumers to ramp up slowly. Brands that win pick fibers and blends people can take daily without discomfort, such as acacia, partially hydrolyzed guar gum, resistant dextrins, and arabinoxylan.

Investor takeaway: ask every fiber brand what fiber it uses, at what dose, and what its 90-day repeat rate looks like. Those three answers tell you whether the product works and whether people stick with it.

Fiber that is intact in whole foods (beans, whole grains, fruit, vegetables) always counts. Isolated or synthetic fibers only count once the FDA has reviewed evidence of a health benefit. Functional descriptions summarize the general nutrition literature; check specific clinical claims before relying on them.

Regulation

Regulation decides what a brand can put on the front of the box, which decides what it can charge. Here's what matters.

How the FDA defines fiber

Since the 2016 Nutrition Facts overhaul, dietary fiber has two parts. Fiber that is intrinsic and intact in plants counts automatically. Isolated or synthetic fiber only counts if the FDA has determined it has a beneficial physiological effect. The same rule raised the Daily Value from 25 to 28 grams.

  • May 2016New definition published with 7 approved isolated fibers, including psyllium, beta-glucan, pectin, and guar gum.
  • June 20188 more added through citizen petitions, including inulin, resistant starch, polydextrose, resistant dextrin, GOS, and mixed plant cell wall fibers.
  • March 2019Cross-linked phosphorylated RS4 added.
  • January 2020Glucomannan (konjac) added.
  • December 2021Acacia (gum arabic) added.

Why it matters: a new fiber ingredient without FDA recognition can't count toward the grams on the label. A petition with clinical data is itself a moat.

Label math

With a 28 g Daily Value, a product needs 10% DV (about 2.8 g per serving) to say "good source of fiber" and 20% DV (about 5.6 g) to say "high" or "excellent source."

Psyllium and oat beta-glucan carry an FDA-authorized heart health claim. Metamucil's label cites 7 grams of soluble fiber per day from psyllium as part of a heart-healthy diet.

2025–2030 Dietary Guidelines

Released January 7, 2026, the new guidelines push "real food," prioritize fiber-rich whole grains, and call for sharply cutting highly processed foods. That helps intact-fiber brands and is a quiet headwind for fortified snacks built on isolated fibers. Stanford's Christopher Gardner noted fiber got relatively little attention in the new guidelines.

"GLP-1 friendly" labels

USDA approved Conagra's and Nestlé's GLP-1 friendly labels because they sit alongside factual protein and fiber statements. Many brands still avoid the term on pack, and some advisors tell clients to lead with benefits instead.

Consumer demand

22% of U.S. shoppers rank high fiber among their top three food purchase factors, up from 17% in 2021. Datassential found 60% of Gen Z want high-fiber food and drink.

Search and social

Google search interest in "fibermaxxing" jumped 115% over a 90-day stretch this summer. The hashtag passed 500 million TikTok views in late 2025.

Retail

Whole Foods named fiber a top 2026 trend, and Thrive Market reported a 30% rise in fiber-related searches over the past year.

Growth outlook

Where the growth is heading. Projections apply each research firm's published CAGR to its 2026 base; they show direction and relative pace, not precise dollar forecasts.

Fastest growth: formats

Fiber gummies are forecast to grow at over 11% a year, nearly three times legacy psyllium. Taste and convenience are where share is moving.

Biggest pool: ingredients

Fiber and prebiotic ingredients are projected to more than double by 2036. Every fortified snack and drink is a customer, and consolidation (Ingredion and Tate & Lyle) shows strategic value.

Most open: GLP-1 and tracking

GLP-1 companion products and fiber tracking combine strong pull with few scaled leaders, the best setup for early-stage investment.

Notable voices

Investment thesis

Fiber will overtake the CPG supplement space, pulled forward by GLP-1 adoption, endurance training, rising chronic disease, and AI tracking that makes intake visible. The winners make clinically credible fiber easy to tolerate every day through formulation or ingredient IP, not grams alone.

GLP-1 support is the wedge

Co-sell fiber through telehealth and GLP-1 channels as a packaged support system rather than fighting for shelf share.

Ingredient IP is the moat

David buying Epogee and Ingredion buying Tate & Lyle both say the same thing: owning the fiber matters.

Format beats grams

Grüns and Poppi grew by solving taste and habit first. Gummies are the fastest-growing fiber format.

Tracking makes fiber visible

Fiber needs to become a number people watch in their apps, the way protein already is.

Athletes open the conversation

Marathon and Hyrox culture makes gut issues discussable and gives fiber a performance story.

Clinical needs prove tolerance

IBS-friendly, low-FODMAP, and recovery formats extend fiber past wellness shoppers.

Investment criteria

  • Named fiber and dose that clears 2.8 g per serving
  • Clinical data or an FDA-recognized fiber
  • 90 and 180-day repeat
  • Subscription retention
  • Retail velocity per SKU, store, and week
  • CAC payback
  • Amazon rank in fiber subcategories
  • A GLP-1 or clinical channel partner

Outside the thesis

  • Single-SKU fiber powders with no formulation IP
  • GLP-1 telehealth without a nutrition layer
  • Prebiotic products with doses too low to deliver a benefit
  • Fortified snacks whose only story is "added fiber" under the new processed-food scrutiny

Open opportunities

Fiber tracking built into the AI nutrition apps people already use. GLP-1 companion products with clinical dosing and a prescriber channel. Diverse fiber blends that lean into the shift from more fiber to more kinds of fiber. Beverages with a meaningful dose, well above the 2 g that got Poppi sued. Kids' fiber, where the deficiency starts.
