Thesis: non-alc and the moderation economy

Moderation is the market

Americans are drinking less than at any point in Gallup's 90-year record, and big alcohol is buying its way into what replaces the drink. This is the investor's view of non-alcoholic beer, spirits, functional social tonics, and the hemp THC reset.

Regulation

Why drinking is falling

The decline is broad, generational, and tied to health beliefs, which makes it more durable than a Dry January spike.

The numbers

Gallup found 54% of U.S. adults drank in 2025, below the prior low of 55% set in 1958 and down from 62% in 2023. Adults 18 to 34 fell to 50%. Women dropped 11 points to 51%, faster than men.

For the first time, a majority (53%) say even one or two drinks a day hurts your health, and Gallup found no sign that marijuana is replacing alcohol.

Who actually buys non-alc

More than 90% of non-alcoholic buyers also buy alcohol, according to NIQ. The customer is a moderator, not an abstainer, which is why NA sits next to beer, wine, and spirits rather than replacing them.

Live Nation found 6 in 10 fans switch between alcoholic and non-alcoholic drinks during an event. Occasions, not identity, drive the purchase.

Beer is losing occasions

IWSR has U.S. beer volume down 3% a year from 2019 to 2024 and forecasts the same decline through 2029.

Liquor stores are shrinking

NIQ shows U.S. liquor store sales fell 3.4% in 2025 to $110B, with NA and RTDs the exceptions.

Functional is mainstream

Functional beverage sales rose 147% over four years to $31.3B (NIQ), so NA drinks now compete on what they do, not just what they lack.

Regulation

Two rule changes shape the next three years: one shuts a door, the other quietly opens one.

The hemp THC reset

  • November 2025The bill ending the federal shutdown redefined hemp, capping hemp-derived products at 0.4 mg of THC per container.
  • 2026States and brands form coalitions, such as Colorado's THC Beverage Coalition with Cycling Frog, to push for a regulated framework.
  • Mid-November 2026The limit takes effect, making most hemp THC drinks federally unlawful unless Congress acts.

Why it matters: shelf space, distributor attention, and consumer demand built by THC drinks will look for a compliant home. Functional tonics are the obvious one.

Health messaging is moving against alcohol

The former U.S. Surgeon General called for cancer warning labels on alcohol, and Gallup shows most Americans now believe moderate drinking is harmful. Notably, the 2025 to 2030 Dietary Guidelines dropped their specific limits on alcohol intake, so the shift is coming from consumers more than from policy.

Functional claims

Adaptogens and nootropics are sold as foods and beverages, so brands describe effects like relaxation without disease claims. Expect more scrutiny of doses and ingredient safety as the category grows.

Market and outlook

Projections apply each source's published growth rate; they show direction and pace, not precise forecasts.

Fastest growth: RTD and spirits

Non-alcoholic RTDs grew 167% in 2024 and NA spirits near 70%, from small bases.

Biggest pool: NA beer

About 80% of NA dollars sit in beer, and IWSR expects 18% annual volume growth in the U.S. through 2029.

Most open: functional and live events

Social tonics with real distribution at venues and festivals have strong pull and few scaled leaders.

Who's shaping the conversation

Investment thesis

Moderation is the market. The winners give people a reason to say yes at the moment they would have ordered a drink: a felt effect, a credible social occasion, and distribution wherever alcohol is sold. Strategic alcohol companies are the natural buyers, so the best bets already have one on the cap table.

Functional beats imitation

Tonics that create a feeling outgrow spirit clones that only remove alcohol and charge spirit prices.

Venues are the distribution unlock

Live Nation and on-premise deals put brands in the exact moment of choice.

Strategic venture arms are the exit

Constellation's stake-then-acquire pattern with HOPWTR is the playbook to underwrite.

The THC reset frees shelf space

Compliant tonics can capture demand built by hemp drinks after November 2026.

Women are leading the shift

Women's drinking fell faster than men's, making female-founded and female-focused brands a natural fit.

Cans over bottles

RTD formats grow fastest and fit both grocery and venues.

Investment criteria

  • Velocity per store per week
  • 90-day repeat
  • Share of sales in alcohol channels
  • On-premise and venue doors
  • A strategic partner or investor
  • Transparent functional doses
  • Gross margin after slotting

Outside the thesis

  • Brands that depend on hemp THC with no plan for November 2026
  • Premium NA spirit clones with weak repeat
  • DTC-only brands without retail or venue distribution

Open opportunities

Functional tonics aimed at women, who are cutting back fastest. Compliant relaxation drinks positioned for the post-ban THC customer. Venue-first brands built with promoters and hospitality groups. Better NA wine, where the taste gap still leaves room for a breakout.
